# Endangered language

## Definition

An endangered language is one at risk of falling out of use because it is not being passed on to younger generations.

### Why it matters in design systems

A language is considered endangered when its community of speakers shrinks and children stop learning it as a mother tongue, putting it on a path toward no longer being spoken. The clearest warning sign is the breakdown of intergenerational transmission: a language is endangered when its speakers "cease to pass it on from one generation to the next."<sup>1</sup> This is about communities and the knowledge, identity, and culture carried in a language, not a statistic, and the people working to keep a language alive are the authority on its situation. Bodies like UNESCO grade endangerment along a [scale](unesco-endangerment-scale.md), but the grading is a tool for those efforts, not a verdict on a community. For design work the consequence is direct: whether a language can be written and read on modern devices at all is part of what decides its future, so it is a responsibility, not an optional nicety.

### Example

A language spoken fluently only by a small number of elderly people, with no children currently learning it, would generally be considered endangered. (Illustrative only; the status of any specific language must come from the community and specialist sources.)

### Common mistake

Treating endangerment as a number, or assuming "few speakers" means "not worth supporting." Vitality is about transmission and use, not just headcount, and digital support can be part of what helps a language continue rather than a reward for languages that are already secure.

### In practice

* **Digital support is part of revitalization:** encoding, fonts, keyboards, and input methods determine whether a language can be written and read on modern devices at all. Several scripts in this glossary exist precisely to write languages under pressure (see [Ol Chiki](../writing-systems-and-scripts/ol-chiki.md), [Warang Citi](../writing-systems-and-scripts/warang-citi.md), [Wancho script](../writing-systems-and-scripts/wancho-script.md), [Syloti Nagri](../writing-systems-and-scripts/syloti-nagri.md), and [Canadian Aboriginal Syllabics](../writing-systems-and-scripts/canadian-aboriginal-syllabics.md)), and [Unicode](../../programming-terms/text-for-digital-products-and-the-web/unicode.md) encoding plus real [font coverage](../../programming-terms/text-for-digital-products-and-the-web/font-coverage.md) is what makes them usable in products.
* **Follow the community's lead:** decisions about [orthography](../writing-systems-and-scripts/orthography.md), naming, and representation belong to speakers and the people working with them. Treat language status, [autonym](autonym.md)s, and vitality as expert- and community-verified, never assumed.
* **State no figures you have not sourced:** speaker counts and endangerment classifications are easy to get wrong and consequential when wrong. Cite a specialist source or leave it to the cohort.
